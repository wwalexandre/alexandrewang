Import document checker

Before a shipment is released, someone has to check that the invoice, packing list, shipping list and certificates of analysis tell the same story. I wrote a Python script that does this check, including on scanned documents, and delivers a ready report.

Python · pandasPyMuPDFTesseract OCR · OpenCVDemo with fictional data

Why it matters

International trade is a chain of deadlines. A quantity that does not match, a repeated batch or a short expiry found too late turns into cargo stuck at the terminal, storage costs and a risk of running out of product at the end of the line.

The manual check cross-references four or five documents line by line. It is slow, tiring and depends on who is checking that day.

How it works

Three result levels

  • FAIL a mismatch that must go back to the supplier before approval.
  • WARNING something to check by eye, for example a doubtful OCR reading.
  • OK the check passed.

The 12 checks

CodeWhat it checks
AConsistent header across documents: exporter, invoice and order numbers, bill to, ship to, contact.
A2Importer's tax ID (CNPJ) present, with a valid check digit and the same on every document.
BQuantity per SKU: invoice vs. shipping list.
CQuantity per family: shipping list vs. packing list, and invoice grand total vs. packing list.
DInvoice values: quantity x unit price = line amount.
EPacking list weights: sum of lines vs. declared totals.
FCartons: shipping list vs. packing list, including by carton size.
GBatches: quantity equal to the number of serials, no duplicated serial, no batch under two product codes.
HMinimum shelf life on arrival (18 months by default) and recent manufacturing.
ICertificate of analysis batch by batch: batch present, code, serials and expiry matching the shipping list.
JFinal signed documents (PDF) vs. spreadsheets: invoice number, quantities, cartons and weights.
KRegulatory import approval: a batch made before the product's approval date is blocked.

Pieces of the logic

Simplified versions of two parts of the script, written for this page.

Reading a page: direct text or OCR

def page_text(page):
    text = page.get_text()
    if len(text.strip()) > 50:          # digital PDF: the text is already there
        return text
    pix = page.get_pixmap(dpi=300)        # scanned PDF: turn it into an image
    img = np.frombuffer(pix.samples, np.uint8).reshape(pix.h, pix.w, pix.n)
    gray = cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_RGB2GRAY)
    _, bw = cv2.threshold(gray, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
    return pytesseract.image_to_string(bw)

Minimum shelf life and recent manufacturing

def check_expiry(batch, arrival_date, min_months=18, max_mfg_months=6):
    remaining = months_between(arrival_date, batch.expiry)
    if remaining < min_months:
        return "FAIL", f"Batch {batch.number} arrives with {remaining} months (minimum {min_months})"
    if months_between(batch.manufactured, arrival_date) > max_mfg_months:
        return "WARNING", f"Batch {batch.number} made more than {max_mfg_months} months ago"
    return "OK", f"Batch {batch.number} within limits"

What I learned

  • OCR fails in predictable ways. The letter O becomes zero, I becomes 1, a colored stamp becomes noise. That is why a doubtful reading becomes a WARNING, not a FAIL.
  • A cache per page makes a difference. Running again after fixing a document takes seconds, because only new pages go through OCR.
  • The invoice date is not always the shipping date. The real departure date is on the transport document, and that changes the shelf life math.

About the code: the original script works with real supplier documents and is not public. The snippets and the report on this page were recreated with fictional data.
