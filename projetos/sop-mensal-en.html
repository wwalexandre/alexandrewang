Monthly S&OP, slide by slide

The 4 weeks of the cycle

The deck is not built the night before the meeting. Each week has a forum, and each forum hands part of the deck to the next one.

  1. Week 1

    Close and data

    Who: Planning and BI

    Output: month closed, with sales, inventory, backorder and pipeline. Slides 1 to 5.

  2. Week 2

    Demand Review

    Who: Demand, Sales and Marketing

    Output: consensus forecast, events and risks. Slides 6 to 10.

  3. Week 3

    Supply Review

    Who: Supply, Production, Procurement and Import

    Output: purchase and production plan and capacity gaps. Slides 11 to 15.

  4. Week 4

    Pre-S&OP and executive meeting

    Who: Finance and leadership

    Output: approved plan and recorded decisions. Slides 16 to 18, plus the summary on slide 3.

Whatever does not close in a week goes up as a decision for the executive meeting. That is why the last slide is about decisions, and slide 4 next month checks whether they were carried out.

01Opening

Cover

Question it answers
Which cycle is this meeting, and what is the last closed month?
What it needs
The cycle month, the closing month and the company or business unit. Here: November cycle, October close.
Where the data comes from
The S&OP calendar for the year, agreed with Finance.
Common mistake
Mixing up the cycle month and the data month. Readers need to know right away that October is the last month with actual sales.
02Opening

How the cycle works

Question it answers
Who decides what, and in which week?
What it needs
The 4 steps, who joins each one and what each meeting hands to the next.
Where the data comes from
The process design: calendar and attendee list for each forum.
Common mistake
Skipping the Supply Review and taking the forecast straight to leadership. They get demand without knowing whether it can be supplied.
03Opening

Executive summary

Question it answers
What changed this month, and what does leadership need to decide today?
What it needs
4 numbers (sales attainment year to date, accuracy, average coverage and backorder), 3 short messages and the decisions needed.
Where the data comes from
The deck itself. It is the last slide I build, and every number has to match its source slide.
Common mistake
Messages that only describe the chart. "Sales 2% below" does not help; "line D below its launch curve, review the launch forecast" does.
04Opening

Actions from last month

Question it answers
Was what we agreed last cycle actually done?
What it needs
Action, owner, due date and status. A late action that does not close before the executive meeting becomes a decision point.
Where the data comes from
The minutes of last month's executive meeting.
Common mistake
Never coming back to the actions. Without this slide, the same decision gets made again every month.
05Demand

Sales actual vs forecast

Question it answers
Did we sell what we planned this year?
What it needs
Actual and forecast year to date, the gap, the plan still open until December and attainment by line.
Where the data comes from
Invoiced sales from the ERP and the frozen forecast for each month, kept in the planning system or in a snapshot table.
Common mistake
Comparing against the latest forecast, which was already adjusted toward the actual. The yardstick is the forecast that was valid when the month started.
How to calculate
Attainment = actual ÷ forecast. In the template, green from 97%, orange between 90% and 97% and red below 90%.
06Demand

Forecast accuracy and bias

Question it answers
Is the forecast right? And when it misses, does it always miss the same way?
What it needs
Monthly MAPE at lag 1 and lag 3, bias by line and a plain reading: balanced, over-forecasting or under-forecasting.
Where the data comes from
Forecast snapshots saved every month (without them there is no lag) and actual sales by SKU.
Common mistake
Measuring MAPE on the company total. One SKU above offsets another below and the error disappears. Here it is calculated by SKU and month.
How to calculate
MAPE = Σ|actual − forecast| ÷ Σ actual and Bias = Σ(forecast − actual) ÷ Σ actual. Lag 1 is the forecast made one month before; lag 3, three months before. I read bias between −5% and +5% as balanced.
07Demand

Statistical vs adjusted (FVA)

Question it answers
Did the team's adjustment improve or hurt the statistical forecast?
What it needs
MAPE of the statistical and final forecast by line, the FVA of each and where the adjustment made things worse.
Where the data comes from
The statistical and final forecasts saved side by side in the same snapshot, in the planning system or the team's table.
Common mistake
Using negative FVA to blame people. It points to the assumption to review: here, the line C promotion and the line E phase-out.
How to calculate
FVA = statistical MAPE − final MAPE, in percentage points. Positive: the adjustment helped. Negative: the statistical forecast alone would have missed less.
08Demand

Forecast vs forecast

Question it answers
What changed in the plan since last cycle, and why?
What it needs
The current cycle against the previous one for the next 6 months and the largest increases and cuts by SKU, each with its reason.
Where the data comes from
Two forecast snapshots: this cycle's and last cycle's.
Common mistake
Showing the change without the reason. Every big swing needs someone who can explain where it came from.
09Demand

Launches, promotions and phase-outs

Question it answers
Which events change volume in the coming months?
What it needs
SKU, event type, start, volume over the next 6 months and status (approved, under review, proposed).
Where the data comes from
Marketing, for launches and promotions, and the portfolio committee, for phase-outs.
Common mistake
A launch with no analog product. Without the curve of a similar item, the launch forecast is a guess.
10Demand

Risks and opportunities

Question it answers
What could make the forecast miss, up or down?
What it needs
Event, volume, probability, weighted volume and owner, with opportunities and risks side by side.
Where the data comes from
The Demand Review, with Sales, Marketing, Import and Production.
Common mistake
Putting the risk straight into the forecast. It stays outside the number and only goes in when its probability passes the agreed threshold.
11Supply

Inventory coverage (MOH)

Question it answers
How many months of sales does the stock cover, and where is it outside the band?
What it needs
By line and SKU: warehouse, in transit, total, average forecast for the next 3 months, MOH, status, last month's MOH and the action requested.
Where the data comes from
The month-end stock position in the ERP (in SAP, MB52 or MMBE), open purchase orders in transit and the cycle forecast.
Common mistake
Dividing by past sales. Coverage looks forward: today's stock against the forecast for the coming months.
How to calculate
MOH = (warehouse + in transit) ÷ average forecast for the next 3 months. Template band: 1.5 to 4 months. Below is stockout risk; above, excess and write-off risk.
12Supply

Channel inventory (SIT)

Question it answers
Are distributors buying to sell or buying to stock up?
What it needs
Days of channel inventory month by month, with the target band and a reading of what to do with next month's sell-in.
Where the data comes from
The stock and sell-out report distributors send every month.
Common mistake
Looking only at sell-in. Pushing volume into a full channel turns into lower orders or returns in the following months.
How to calculate
Channel days = distributor stock ÷ average sell-out of the last 3 months × 30. Template band: 30 to 45 days.
13Supply

Backorder

Question it answers
How much customer demand is stuck, and where?
What it needs
Units, value, customers and lines affected, the monthly trend and the SKUs with the most backorder.
Where the data comes from
The open order book in the ERP.
Common mistake
Showing only the total. Each SKU needs its cause (capacity, a late import or a low forecast) and the expected date to ship.
14Supply

Import pipeline

Question it answers
Where is each shipment, and when does it become available stock?
What it needs
Order, SKU, quantity, ETA and the step of each shipment: ready at origin, in transit, customs, receiving and available.
Where the data comes from
Import follow-up with the freight forwarder and the customs broker.
Common mistake
Using the ETA as the date stock is available. Between the vessel arriving and the product being released there is still customs and receiving, and that is where delays usually show up.
15Supply

Purchase and production plan

Question it answers
How much to buy and make in the next 3 months, and can the plant handle it?
What it needs
Suggested order by line and month, available hours against planned hours and the utilization of each month.
Where the data comes from
The cycle forecast, projected stock and plant capacity in hours, taken from the MPS.
Common mistake
Closing the plan without checking capacity. Here December goes over 100%, and the choice between building ahead and approving overtime has to come out of this meeting.
How to calculate
Order = max(0; 1.5 × max(fcst N; fcst N+1) + fcst N − projected stock), where 1.5 is the target coverage in months. Utilization = planned hours ÷ available hours.
16Finance

Revenue vs budget

Question it answers
Is the volume sold turning into the budgeted revenue?
What it needs
Revenue and budget year to date, attainment, how much of the full-year budget is already done and the gap by line.
Where the data comes from
Revenue from the accounting close and the annual budget, together with Finance.
Common mistake
Volume and revenue telling different stories with no explanation. When volume is close to plan and revenue is not, the answer is usually mix or price, and the slide has to say which.
How to calculate
Attainment = revenue ÷ budget for the period and Gap = revenue − budget, by line and in total.
17Finance

Inventory at risk (SLOB)

Question it answers
How much stock could turn into a loss, and what do we do with each bucket?
What it needs
Value by expiry or stock-age bucket and an action with an owner for each bucket.
Where the data comes from
Stock by batch with its expiry or receipt date, valued at cost.
Common mistake
Showing only the value. Without an action and an owner, the 90-day bucket becomes a write-off next month.
18Closing

Decisions and escalations

Question it answers
What leaves this meeting approved?
What it needs
Decision needed, options, recommendation, owner and due date.
Where the data comes from
The gaps left over from the Demand Review and the Supply Review.
Common mistake
Bringing a problem with no recommendation. Leadership decides faster when the team arrives with a proposal.

How to adapt it to your industry

The structure of the 18 slides works for any company that plans inventory. What changes is how much weight each slide gets.

Pharma

Shelf life drives SLOB, and the buckets are by expiry. Quality release is part of the lead time, and a launch depends on regulatory approval. Tenders matter more than promotions.

Consumer goods

Sell-in vs sell-out and channel inventory (SIT) become the core of the Demand Review. Promotions and trade marketing are the biggest forecast risks.

Retail

The channel slide becomes in-store stockouts, turnover and coverage by store. Demand is by SKU and store, and replenishment weighs more than production.

Manufacturing

Capacity, setups and raw materials rule. The production plan becomes the main slide, with sequencing and material constraints.

Glossary

S&OP
Sales and Operations Planning: the monthly cycle that brings demand, supply and finance into one plan.
IBP
Integrated Business Planning: S&OP with finance and portfolio built in, over a longer horizon.
MBR
Monthly Business Review: the executive meeting of the month, where the plan is approved.
Demand Review
Meeting where demand, sales and marketing agree on the consensus forecast.
Supply Review
Meeting where supply, production and procurement check whether the forecast fits capacity and stock.
Lag
How many months before the forecast was made. Lag 1: one month before the measured month.
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error, weighted by volume. Lower is better.
Bias
Whether the forecast keeps missing high (positive) or low (negative).
FVA
Forecast Value Added: how much the team's adjustment improved the statistical forecast.
MOH
Months on Hand: how many months of forecast the stock covers.
SIT
Stock in Trade: inventory sitting in the channel (distributors and customers), measured in days of sell-out.
Backorder
An accepted customer order that has not shipped yet.
SLOB
Slow-moving and Obsolete: stock that is not moving or is close to expiry.
NPI
New Product Introduction: a product launch.
EOL
End of Life: a product being phased out.

Where I did this

  • Sanofi (2026): Demand Planning in the IBP cycle. Demand Review in Kinaxis, MAPE, Bias and FVA at lag 1 and lag 3, channel inventory (SIT) in the MBR and ODR and SLOB dashboards for the forums.
  • Hering (2025 to 2026): stockouts, turnover and coverage in the company's own stores, with automated bulletins for store managers and coordinators.
  • Hypera Pharma (2024 to 2025): the MPS cycle and the monthly presentation of the 3-month production plan and sequencing at the MBR.
  • Abbott (2022 to 2024): production. KPIs for the technical team and an A3 project that gained capacity, the basis for the plan and utilization slide.
